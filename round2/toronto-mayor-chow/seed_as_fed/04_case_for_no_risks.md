# The case for NO and the risks: why Brad Bradford could unseat Chow

Bradford's support rose from 39% in Liaison's poll of September 4-5, 2026 to 49% in its poll of October 18-19, 2026, and Chow's Nanos lead shrank from about 10 points (September 30 - October 4, 2026) to about 3 points (October 15-18, 2026). Two IVR polls, Mainstreet (October 16-18, 2026) and Liaison (October 18-19, 2026), plus Build Canada (October 11-16, 2026), had Bradford narrowly ahead. The mood favours change: in Build Canada's October 11-16, 2026 poll, 60% wanted a major change in direction and 61% said Toronto was worse than four years ago, and a Canada Pulse poll for CityNews (September 14-24, 2026) found 55% saying it was time for a new mayor. Nanos said on October 13, 2026 that Bradford picked up some of Chris Alexander's voters after Alexander quit on October 6, 2026. Liaison (October 18-19, 2026) had Bradford leading in North York (56% to 42%) and Etobicoke (63% to 35%), and Nanos (October 15-18, 2026) had him ahead among voters aged 35 and over. Several federal Liberal MPs backed him by October 5, 2026, as did Lisa Raitt and Shopify CEO Tobias Lutke (listed October 23, 2026). At the October 22, 2026 debate he attacked Chow's 9.5% and 6.9% tax hikes and promised a one-year tax freeze. Risks for Chow: the Pollara polls were paid for by a union that endorsed her; Nanos still found 16% undecided (October 15-18, 2026); and in the June 26, 2023 by-election the late-rising runner-up Ana Bailao won 32.46%, above the 30% in Mainstreet's final poll.
