# Background and timeline of the 2026 Toronto mayoral race

Olivia Chow became mayor of Toronto by winning the by-election of June 26, 2023 with 37.16% of the vote, ahead of Ana Bailao at 32.46%; that by-election was called after John Tory resigned on February 17, 2023, and Brad Bradford finished it with only 1.28%. On October 1, 2025, Bradford, the councillor for Ward 19 Beaches-East York and a former urban planner, became the first notable candidate to declare for the 2026 race. Under Chow, residential property taxes rose 9.5% in 2024 and 6.9% in 2025, and council adopted a smaller 2.2% increase for 2026 on February 10, 2026. John Tory announced on March 3, 2026 that he would not run again. Chow formally entered the race on May 25, 2026. Former federal Conservative minister Chris Alexander registered on July 29, 2026 and drew about 9-10% in polls fielded between August 4 and September 27, 2026. Nominations closed on August 21, 2026, leaving 53 names on the mayoral ballot. On September 16, 2026, Premier Doug Ford publicly praised Chow, and his office said this was not a formal endorsement. Alexander ended his campaign on October 6, 2026 without endorsing anyone, but his name stays on the ballot. Advance voting ran from October 6 to October 11, 2026, and the last televised Chow-Bradford debate took place on October 22, 2026. Election day is Monday, October 26, 2026, and since October 6, 2026 the contest has been a two-way race between Chow and Bradford.
