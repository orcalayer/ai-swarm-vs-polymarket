# How and when the question will be decided

Toronto votes on Monday, October 26, 2026; polls are open 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern time, i.e. until midnight UTC on October 27, 2026. The mayor is elected by simple plurality with no runoff, because Ontario's Bill 218, passed on November 20, 2020, removed the ranked-ballot option; Chow won the June 26, 2023 by-election with only 37.16%. The ballot for October 26, 2026 lists 53 names, including Chris Alexander, who withdrew on October 6, 2026 too late to be removed. About 2.14 million people are eligible to vote on October 26, 2026, per the City's fact sheet. A record 222,550 people voted in advance polls from October 6 to October 11, 2026, and mail-in ballots were due by noon on October 14, 2026. Total turnout was 725,333 in the June 26, 2023 by-election and 563,124 in the October 24, 2022 election. Unofficial ward-by-ward results covering advance, mail-in and election-day votes will be posted after polls close on the night of October 26, 2026. A legal summary of April 25, 2023 noted that Ontario's Municipal Elections Act makes a recount automatic only in a tie; otherwise a council or board resolution or a court order is needed. Under the resolution rules as published on October 24, 2026, the question resolves to whoever becomes the next mayor as a result of this election, by a consensus of credible reporting, with official City of Toronto information used in case of ambiguity; if no result is known by June 30, 2027, 11:59 PM ET, it resolves "Other".
