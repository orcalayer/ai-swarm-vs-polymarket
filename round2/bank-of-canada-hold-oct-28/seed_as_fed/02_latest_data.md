# Canada: latest data before the 28 October 2026 decision

Statistics Canada reported on 19 October 2026 that CPI rose 3.1% year over year in September 2026, up from 3.0% in August and slightly below the 3.2% economists expected. In that 19 October 2026 report, gasoline was up 27.8% on the year, while CPI excluding gasoline rose 2.2%, down from 2.4%. The Bank's core measures eased in the same 19 October 2026 release: CPI-trim to 1.8% from 1.9%, CPI-median to 1.9% from 2.0%.

The Labour Force Survey of 9 October 2026 showed employment fell by 68,000 in September 2026, after a 42,000 drop in August, and unemployment rose to 6.5% from 6.4%. In the same 9 October 2026 survey, participation fell to 64.8% and average hourly wages were up 2.3% on the year.

Statistics Canada reported on 28 August 2026 that real GDP grew at a 3.3% annualized pace in the second quarter of 2026. By 29 September 2026, monthly GDP was flat in July and a flash estimate showed +0.2% for August. Economists polled by Reuters on 21-23 October 2026 estimated growth of about 1.9% in the third quarter and 1.3% in the fourth.

Brent crude settled at US$113.17 a barrel on 22 October 2026, its highest since late April, and Reuters reported oil at US$117 on 23 October 2026. The Bank of Canada's daily rate was 1.3993 Canadian dollars per US dollar on 23 October 2026, versus 1.4097 on 19 October 2026.

The next jobs report (6 November 2026) and next CPI (16 November 2026) both come after the decision.
