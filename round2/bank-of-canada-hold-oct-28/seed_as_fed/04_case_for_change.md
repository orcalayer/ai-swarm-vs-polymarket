# The case for a change on 28 October 2026 (mainly a hike)

On 17 October 2026, at IMF meetings in Bangkok, Governor Tiff Macklem said the Bank expected to raise its inflation forecast because oil was far above its July assumption: "If oil prices stay around US$100, we expect inflation to gradually drift up from where it is today: 3 per cent." He added on 17 October 2026 that the risk inflation broadens "has gone up". On 21 September 2026 he said the Bank did not want "to be too slow to respond", and its 2 September 2026 deliberations said energy spillovers "could require a monetary policy response".

Bank surveys released on 19 October 2026 showed about half of firms planning to pass on cost increases, nearly a quarter unable to say when inflation returns to 2%, and consumers' long-term inflation expectations rising. Brent settled at US$113.17 on 22 October 2026, while the July 2026 outlook assumed about US$75, per The Hub on 19 October 2026. The Fed hiked on 16 September 2026, and the ECB's second 2026 hike took effect the same day.

In a Reuters poll of 21-23 October 2026, 19 of 34 economists expected a 25 basis-point hike to 2.50%, reversing the previous poll, and 16 of 29 saw another by end-March 2027. Scotiabank's Derek Holt said on 19 October 2026: "It's not a slam dunk call but there is probably enough supporting evidence for a hike." Desjardins and BMO switched to hike calls on 19 October 2026.

A cut looks remote: no forecast reviewed from 9-23 October 2026 called for one, though job losses, tariffs and sub-2% core inflation would be the arguments.
