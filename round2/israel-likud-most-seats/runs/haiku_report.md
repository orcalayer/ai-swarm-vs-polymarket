# Future Preview: Who Wins the Most Seats in Israel's October 27, 2026 Knesset Election?

> The simulated future points to a close race in which Likud is more likely than not to be overtaken by Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar in most polls, so the probability that Likud wins the most seats is below even, at roughly 35 to 40 out of 100.

---

## Divergent Polling Landscape: Likud Versus Yashar

The simulated polling landscape in the final week before the October 27, 2026 vote is split, and the split is largely a question of which pollster is asking. Read as a whole, the record does not show a clear lead for either list. It shows two camps of pollsters producing opposite results from similar fieldwork windows.

**Pollsters that put Likud ahead**

The clearest Likud-leading results come from pollsters aligned with the right. Channel 14 and i24NEWS account for most of the Likud advantage:

> "A Channel 14 poll on 22 October 2026 projected 30 seats for Likud and 20 seats for Yashar."

> "Direct Polls conducted a poll for i24NEWS on 20 October 2026 that projected Likud at 26 seats and Yashar at 20 seats."

Across the 41 published polls with fieldwork from September 27 to October 22, Likud led 14, and every one of those leads came from Channel 14 or i24NEWS. The same record notes that Channel 14's polling is run by Shlomo Filber, a former aide to Netanyahu's campaign, which is a reason to read these numbers with caution.

**Pollsters that put Yashar ahead**

The mainstream and broadcast polls show Yashar ahead, usually by one to three seats:

> "Channel 13's poll published on 23 October 2026 projected 22 seats for Yashar, placing Yashar first."

> "Kan 11's poll published on 23 October 2026 projected 20 seats for Likud, placing Likud second."

> "A Walla poll published on 20 October 2026 projected 23 seats for Yashar and 19 seats for Likud."

> "A Maariv poll by Lazar Research, published on 23 October 2026 after fieldwork from 19 to 22 October 2026, projected 21 seats for Yashar, 19 for Likud, and 15 for Together."

Of the same 41 polls, Yashar led 25. In the three final TV polls on October 23, Likud trailed Yashar by two to three seats. Some results also sit in between: Zman Yisrael reported a 21-21 tie for September 30 to October 1, and Channel 13 reported a 20-20 tie on October 7.

**How the trend moved from September to late October**

The record shows Likud recovering in the aggregate over the period. One aggregator's average has Likud rising "from about 17 seats in September 2026 to about 20 seats" by October 22. Even so, the mainstream polls did not show Likud overtaking Yashar by the final week. The gap narrowed rather than reversing.

**Why the polls disagree**

The simulated record offers three explanations, though none is tested directly:

- **House effects.** The consistent split between Channel 14 and i24NEWS on one side and the mainstream outlets on the other suggests the difference comes from each pollster's sample and weighting rather than from a shift in voters.
- **Historical accuracy.** Commentators disagree about which pollsters to trust. Jewish Insider described Direct Polls as "the most accurate pollster of the previous election," but the record also notes that mainstream pollsters underestimated Likud in 2022, with final polls giving it 30 or 31 seats against the 32 it won.
- **Voter certainty.** A Walla poll published October 20 found that 68% of Likud voters were completely sure of their vote, against 40% of Yashar voters and 23% of Together voters. Yashar's support therefore looks softer, which fits the narrow lead and the chance that some votes drift to allied lists.

**Defections from 2022 Likud voters**

A Channel 12 poll from May 11, 2026 found that 58% of 2022 Likud voters planned to vote Likud again, with 10% planning to vote Together and 6% Yashar. Haaretz reported on October 23 that 56% of religious Zionists who voted Likud in 2022 were leaning toward Itamar Ben-Gvir's or Bezalel Smotrich's parties rather than Likud. Both findings point to leakage from Likud to other right-wing lists, which would limit Likud's share even if its core holds.

**Interview coverage**

The planned agent interviews could not be run because the simulation environment was not active, so this section draws only on the recorded poll results and the sourced commentary. Agent-level explanations of why voters might be more or less committed to either side are therefore not included.

**What the divergence means for the question**

Taken together, the polls point to a near-even contest in which Likud's leads come almost entirely from the pollsters most favourable to it, and mainstream polling puts Yashar slightly ahead with a more committed base. This is consistent with a probability below even for Likud winning the most seats, though the Channel 14 and i24NEWS results and Likud's history of beating its polls in 2022 keep a Likud win within reach.

## Reactions and Actions of Key Agent Groups

The simulated record for the final week shows the main agent groups acting mostly through polls, public statements about voter certainty, and strategic signals about the smaller lists. The record contains no first-person statements from agents on how they planned to behave on election day. The planned interviews with Likud, Yashar, Together and religious Zionist voters failed because the simulation environment was not running. The findings below therefore describe group behaviour as recorded in poll and media reports, and they should be read with that limit in mind.

**Likud's base: high certainty, with leakage at the edges**

Likud's core supporters appear the most settled of the main blocs. A Walla poll published on 20 October 2026 found that "68% of Likud voters were completely sure of their vote, compared with 40% of Yashar voters and 23% of Together voters." The Likud campaign therefore looks less like a mobilisation problem and more like a question of whether its base turns out in full.

The leakage signals are weaker and come from the right rather than the centre. A Channel 12 poll on 11 May 2026 found that "58% of 2022 Likud voters planned to vote Likud again," with 10% planning to vote Together and 6% Yashar. Haaretz reported on 23 October 2026 that "56% of religious Zionists who voted Likud in 2022 did not plan to vote Likud this time and were leaning toward Itamar Ben-Gvir's or Bezalel Smotrich's parties." The religious Zionist defection is the more consequential of the two, since it moves votes to allied right-wing lists rather than to the opposition.

**Yashar and Together: a narrow lead and softer support**

Yashar's position is a lead, but one whose supporters are less certain. The record shows Yashar leading the mainstream polls by one to three seats, and the 40% certainty figure above suggests that lead could move. The panorama record notes that "some of Yashar's soft support could still drift to its own allies; Together gained two seats in the Maariv poll of 23 October 2026." Together's voters are the least certain of the three main groups, with 23% completely sure of their vote, and the Maariv poll put Together at 15 seats.

Benny Gantz's exit has also changed the opposition's arithmetic. The record states that on 19 October 2026 "Benny Gantz pulled Blue and White out of the race after polls kept showing the party below the 3.25% threshold." JC linked a widening of the opposition lead to the dispersal of Gantz's voters, which means those votes are now open to other lists.

**Small lists and the threshold**

Several small lists sat near the threshold in the final days, and the record treats them as a factor in the larger lists' seat counts. On 23 October 2026, "Ofer Winter's party sat near the 3.25% electoral threshold, creating a risk that wasted votes could shift the larger lists by one or two seats," and the Reservists were in the same position. Because a seat or two can decide the outcome in a race this close, these lists' final vote share matters to both Likud and Yashar.

**Media and pollsters interpreting the gap**

Media coverage in the final week centred on the poll split rather than on new campaign events. Jewish Insider described Direct Polls as "the most accurate pollster of the previous election" and called Channel 14's and i24NEWS's polls outliers, a framing that reflects how commentators judged the right-leaning pollsters. The record also notes that mainstream pollsters underestimated Likud in 2022, when their final polls gave it 30 or 31 seats against the 32 it won. Because publication of new polls was banned in the last days, the interpretive debate in the final week was carried by the existing results.

**Gaps in the record**

The simulated record does not show Likud, Yashar or Together rallies, election-day mobilisation drives, or disputes over election logistics in the final week. Diplomatic staff voted on 15–16 October 2026, and their ballots, along with those of soldiers, are counted after the regular count, which could affect the final tally. Beyond these points, the agent-level picture of the last week is incomplete.

## Structural Risks: Ties, Ambiguity and Resolution Timing

The structural rules add little to the headline probability, but they do define how a close result turns into a YES or NO. The main risk is a seat tie, followed by how long the count takes. The simulated record shows no recount, dispute or delay, so the resolution mechanics look routine. The probability sits mainly on the seat count itself.

**Tie-breaking rule**

A seat tie is decided first by the number of valid votes each list received, and then by alphabetical order if those are also equal. The record repeatedly states this rule, but it contains no valid-vote estimates for Likud or Yashar. The tie-breaker therefore cannot be assessed from the simulation, and it would not be known until the official count is complete.

Ties have already appeared in the polling record. Zman Yisrael reported "Likud and Yashar tied at 21 seats" for 30 September to 1 October 2026, and Channel 13 reported "Likud and Yashar tied at 20 seats" on 7 October 2026. The final-week polls show a gap of two to three seats, so a dead heat in the actual count is a live possibility within the margin of error.

**Timing of the official result**

The record sets a clear publication timetable. Under Article 11 of Basic Law: The Knesset, "official results must be published within eight days of election day, by 4 November 2026." The count is staged. "The ballots are counted after the regular count," and the ballots cast by diplomatic staff abroad, who voted on 15–16 October 2026, are also counted after the regular count. Military ballots follow the same sequence.

Election-night television exit polls do not settle the question. They are "projections, not results," and the criteria state that exit polls "do not count toward determining the outcome." The first meaningful signal therefore arrives only as the regular count is tallied, and the final seat allocation depends on the later ballots.

**Resolution cutoff**

The criteria set a hard deadline: "As of 30 June 2027, Likud is not counted as the winner" if the result is still unsettled. The official 4 November publication date falls well inside that window. Unless a dispute or recount extended the process past June 2027, the cutoff is a remote risk. The simulated record contains no evidence of either. A targeted search for recounts, disputes and count delays returned only poll entries, with no reports of any such event.

**Small lists and the threshold**

The most concrete structural risk in the record is the 3.25% threshold. On 23 October 2026, "Ofer Winter's party sat near the 3.25% electoral threshold, creating a risk that wasted votes could shift the larger lists by one or two seats." The Reservists were in the same position, and the record notes that wasted votes from small lists "could move the larger lists by one or two seats." Blue and White left the race on 19 October after polls kept it below the threshold, and JC linked the opposition's gain to the dispersal of those voters. In a race where the gap is two to three seats, a shift of one or two seats from wasted votes could decide the top spot.

**What counts and what does not**

The criteria are narrow, and the record repeatedly confirms them. Only the seats won by Likud's own candidate list count. Bloc totals do not. In the three final TV polls, "Netanyahu's bloc" had 50 seats and the opposition 52 to 56, with "neither reaching the 61-seat majority," but these figures are irrelevant to the question. The same applies to the coalition that eventually governs and to the person asked to form a government.

The New Hope element is a live interpretive point. Likud filed "its own list without a partner party" on 8 September 2026, but its slate "carried New Hope figures including Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar under a Likud-New Hope merger." Because New Hope candidates run on the single Likud list, their seats should count toward Likud's list total. The record does not show any dispute over this classification.

**Poll error as a structural factor**

Poll error runs in both directions and widens the range of plausible counts. In 2022, "mainstream pollsters underestimated Likud," whose final polls gave 30 or 31 seats against the 32 it won, while Direct Polls "gave Likud 34 seats in its poll of 27 October 2022, overshooting" that result. Given the spread between pollsters in 2026, the true outcome could fall anywhere from a clear Yashar win to a clear Likud win. That spread makes a tie or near-tie more likely than any single poll suggests.

**Gaps in the record**

The simulation does not report the final seat totals, valid-vote figures, the counting progress, or any official announcement. The count's outcome is therefore not visible in the record, and the structural analysis above describes the rules and risks rather than an observed resolution.

## Core Trend Forecast and Probability Assessment

The simulated record points to a close race that Yashar is more likely than not to win, so the probability that Likud finishes with the most seats is below even. The judgment rests on the mainstream polls, which consistently show Yashar ahead, and on the narrow gap that leaves room for the outcome to flip. Taking the evidence together, a range of roughly 35 to 40 out of 100 is the most defensible estimate.

**Why Yashar is the more likely first-place list**

The mainstream and broadcast polls from the final week favour Yashar. Across 41 published polls with fieldwork from 27 September to 22 October 2026, Yashar led 25 and Likud led 14. Every Likud lead came from Channel 14 or i24NEWS. The final TV polls on 23 October reinforce the pattern:

> "In the three final TV polls of 23 October 2026, Likud trailed Yashar by 2 to 3 seats."

Individual polls show the same ordering. Channel 13 projected "22 seats for Yashar, placing Yashar first," Channel 12 projected "23 seats for Yashar, placing Yashar first," and Kan 11 projected "22 seats for Yashar, placing Yashar first." The Maariv poll by Lazar Research, with fieldwork from 19 to 22 October, projected "21 seats for Yashar, 19 for Likud, and 15 for Together."

**Why Likud's chances are not negligible**

The record shows a real Likud recovery and a narrow gap. One average has Likud rising "from about 17 seats in September 2026 to about 20 seats" by 22 October. Two polls have ended in a tie, which shows how close the lists are. Zman Yisrael reported "Likud and Yashar tied at 21 seats," and Channel 13 reported "Likud and Yashar tied at 20 seats on 7 October 2026."

The 2022 record adds a reason for caution about the mainstream polls. Mainstream pollsters' final polls "gave Likud 30 or 31 seats, while Likud won 32 seats on 1 November 2022," so they underestimated Likud last time. The right-leaning pollsters were closer to the eventual result on that occasion, though Direct Polls overshot, having "gave Likud 34 seats in its poll of 27 October 2022." That history is why the gap should not be treated as settled.

**The factors that could move the result**

- **Likud's base is more settled.** A Walla poll found that "68% of Likud voters were completely sure of their vote, compared with 40% of Yashar voters and 23% of Together voters." Yashar's lead rests on softer support.
- **Right-wing leakage.** Haaretz reported that "56% of religious Zionists who voted Likud in 2022 did not plan to vote Likud this time." This leakage cuts against Likud's count even if its core holds.
- **Wasted votes near the threshold.** The Reservists and Ofer Winter's party "sat near the 3.25% electoral threshold, creating a risk that wasted votes could shift the larger lists by one or two seats." In a race where the gap is two to three seats, that shift could decide first place.

**How the probability was set**

The estimate starts from the mainstream consensus, which puts Yashar ahead by about two to three seats in the final week. It is adjusted upward for the 2022 underestimate of Likud and for Likud's recovery through September and October, and downward for the softer support of Yashar's voters, the religious Zionist leakage and the wasted-vote risk. The simulated record does not give a probability directly. Taken together, these factors support a range of roughly 35 to 40 out of 100.

**Resolution notes**

The question resolves only on the seats won by Likud's own candidate list. Bloc totals, the coalition that later governs and exit polls do not count. Ties are broken first by valid votes and then alphabetically. The record gives an official publication deadline of 4 November 2026, well inside the 30 June 2027 cutoff, and shows no recount or dispute that would delay the outcome. The main structural risk is therefore the seat count itself, not the timing of the result.
